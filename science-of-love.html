The Three Biological Stages of Love

According to renowned biological anthropologist Dr. Helen Fisher of Rutgers University, romantic love is divided into three distinct brain systems, each driven by specific hormones and neurotransmitters:

1. Lust (Libido & Drive)

Driven primarily by testosterone and estrogen in both men and women, this evolutionary drive sparks physical attraction and reproductive interest.

2. Attraction (The Dopamine Rush)

When people fall in love, their brains release surges of dopamine (the reward neurotransmitter) and norepinephrine (adrenaline-like stimulation). This causes the classic symptoms of being lovesick: intense focus on the partner, loss of appetite, sleeplessness, and butterflies in the stomach. Simultaneously, serotonin levels temporarily drop, mimicking obsessive thoughts.

3. Attachment (Oxytocin & Vasopressin)

While romantic infatuation eventually stabilizes after 12 to 18 months, enduring partnerships transition into the deep attachment phase. Sustained by oxytocin (the cuddle hormone) and vasopressin, this phase fosters emotional safety, trust, long-term commitment, and family bonding.

🧠 Why Algorithmic Tests Are So Popular:

Love calculators tap into humanity's ancient desire to predict attachment outcomes before taking emotional risks. While our algorithms evaluate linguistic and astrological symmetry, daily emotional attunement is what transforms attraction into lasting attachment.

Dr. John Gottman's "Magic 5:1 Ratio"

Renowned relationship psychologist Dr. John Gottman discovered that stable, long-lasting couples maintain a ratio of at least five positive interactions for every single negative interaction during conflict. Small acts of appreciation, gentle humor, and active listening far outweigh initial compatibility scores.
